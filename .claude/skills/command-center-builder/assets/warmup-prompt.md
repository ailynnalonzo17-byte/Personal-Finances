# Warm-up prompt — what the student pastes to start (Phase 0a)

Behind the "Copy the warm-up prompt" button on the Week 4 page. Vera finds out everything she can
alone, **drafts the intake questions and holds them**, and reports facts only. On a live call this
runs at the top while the teacher is still setting up, so that when the student later says "build my
command center" the questions appear immediately instead of after a long silence.

**Same chat for both.** The held draft lives in the conversation, so the warm-up and the build have
to be the same thread. The prompt says so.

---

Hey Vera, get me set up for the Command Center build. Load the command-center-builder skill — if you do not have it, install it from my memory vault or from the .skill file I was given, and tell me which version loaded.

Then find out everything you can on your own, and do not ask me anything yet:

1. Check my prep. If I already ran the line-up step in my pre-work, the whole check is one thing: open my Command Center address and see whether the holding page loads behind my login, and whether it refreshed itself in the last couple of hours. If there is no address yet, fall back to checking that I can sign into Cloudflare, that Zero Trust opens to its own dashboard, and that both CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN and AIRTABLE_TOKEN are in my memory vault's secrets.
2. List which of Airtable, Google Calendar, Slack, Gmail and QuickBooks I have connected, **and the actual accounts behind each one** — which email addresses, which Slack workspaces, which calendar, which QuickBooks company. Not just the service names. You need the account list later and you cannot guess it.
3. Read my Week 1 foundational skills and my vault so you know what my business actually does and how it makes money.
4. Look at my Airtable base. Get the table NAMES first, do not pull the whole schema, it is huge. Pick the handful that look like they carry my business, then read a few actual records from just those so you know which fields exist AND which ones I have really been filling in. If my base is empty, that is fine and normal — do not make it a thing.
5. If QuickBooks is connected, run my P&L and check whether the last three months have income posted. Keep what you find for later; do not bring up money yet.

Then, without showing me any of it, write out the four questions you are going to ask me when I say go, with your recommendation already filled in on every line: which business to build first, which four to six numbers to put on it, whether money in and out belongs on the page, and which of my accounts feeds which business. Hold that message. Do not send it and do not hint at what is in it.

Then report back in about eight lines: the skill version, the prep check, what is connected and what is not, one line on what my business does, which tables hold real data, and the books finding if there is one. Facts only, no suggestions and no questions.

Then stop. Do not start building, and stay in this chat — I will say "build my command center" here when I am ready, and you will send me those questions straight away.
